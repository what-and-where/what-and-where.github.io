What&Where: Demand-Driven Dynamic Object Search
via Vision-Language Model Collaboration

Anonymous
Submitted to KBS 2026

Abstract

Having robots find daily objects according to human demands is essential for intelligent domestic service. However, the underspecified identity of target objects in human high-level demand instructions, compounded by their lack of fixed locations across daily routines, introduces significant challenges to existing object search methods. This paper investigates demand-driven dynamic object search (D-DOS) for everyday household assistance. Accordingly, we propose What&Where, a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-powered collaborative framework that explicitly considers what makes objects suitable for user demands and where the search should be prioritized in the environment. For ``what'' reasoning, the Demand Explicator interprets user demands into functional traits that desired objects should possess, enabling flexible target matching among diverse alternatives. For ``where'' reasoning, the Region Explorer grounds the search region in the global top-down view of the environment, while the Action Exploiter performs local search and revisits region selection as needed, enabling adaptive search in complex household environments. Experiments on the ProcTHOR simulator show that What&Where consistently outperforms strong baselines in both success rate and task efficiency. Real-world evaluations further demonstrate its practical effectiveness in household service scenarios.

Approach

Given high-level demand instructions, our framework employs VLMs as three specialized agents to collaboratively search for demand objects.
